The part nobody prepares you for

The Camino surfaces things. It does not finish them.

You are back, but you are not home yet.

For weeks you had one job. Walk. Eat. Sleep. Walk again. Something came loose out there, and you felt it. Maybe it was grief you had been carrying for years. Maybe it was a decision you had been avoiding. Maybe it was just the quiet, and what the quiet said to you.

Then you flew home. The laundry, the inbox, the same kitchen. Within a month the Camino starts to feel like a holiday you once took, and the thing that came loose quietly settles back into place.

That is not a failure of will. It is what happens to every pilgrim who walks back into an unchanged life with no plan for the thing they are carrying. The walk opens it. The homecoming is where it either takes root or closes over.

The six weeks

What we actually do.

  • Week one

    Arrival

    We start with what you wrote on your intake form, then walk through both frameworks: MAPS, the movements of the journey, and WALK, the daily practice that holds it together between calls.

  • Week two

    The Weight

    What is still sitting on you from before the walk. The Camino surfaced it. This is where we find out exactly what it is, and stop trying to fix it long enough to name it.

  • Week three

    The Practice

    You leave with one daily rhythm you will actually keep. The footsteps mantra: a word paired with every step. Inhale here, exhale now, inhale step, exhale step.

  • Week four

    Relationships

    Who in your life needs to hear what changed, and how to say it. We script that conversation before you have it, so it goes the way you mean it to.

  • Week five

    The Next Mountain

    Not another pilgrimage. The ordinary one: work, family, the thing you have been avoiding. We map it the way you would map a stage. Stages, rest stops, what to carry.

  • Week six

    Integration

    You write your own one-sentence answer to what the Camino did to you. That is your homecoming, and you keep it. Then you lay the stone down.

The walk ends. The pilgrimage does not.

How it works

Two frameworks, used every week.

MAPS, the movements of the journey

Mental
Clarify what you are carrying home, and what you are choosing to let go of.
Awareness
Name the grief, the fear, the unfinished thing, without rushing to fix it.
Physical
One daily rhythm that keeps the road under your feet.
Spiritual IQ
How God might be speaking through this season.
Spiritual EQ
What in your history shapes how you receive it, and where your deepest wound might be your deepest gift.

WALK, the daily practice

W, Wake up
Write your dreams, your feelings, and your intention for the day. Walk toward the sun. Water yourself, sixteen ounces in the first half hour.
A, Accept what is
Stay present. It is all we truly have. Do not replay the past. Do not catastrophize the future.
L, Light a candle
A gratitude candle. Three things you are grateful for, morning and night. Then ten to fifteen minutes in meditation.
K, Know that you are enough
Not when you have fixed it. Now.

Before you ask

The questions people actually ask.

How long ago do I have to have walked?

There is no window. Fresh off the plane is good, because it is all still loud. Three years out is also good, because by then you know which parts stayed. If you are still thinking about it, it is not too late.

I have not walked a Camino yet. Is this for me?

Not this one. This is for pilgrims coming home. If you are preparing to walk, the Wilgrim cohorts are built for that, and I would rather put you in the right room. Write to me and I will point you there.

Do I have to be religious?

No. Two of the five movements are spiritual, and I am a person of faith, so I will not pretend otherwise. But this is your pilgrimage, not mine. Plenty of people do this work without sharing my beliefs and get everything out of it.

What if it is not working?

Then you stop at the end of week two and I refund the rest. You do not have to justify it. I would rather give the money back than keep someone in six weeks that are not helping.

Is this therapy?

No, and it is not a substitute for it. This is coaching: structured, practical, built around what you do next. If what surfaces needs a therapist, I will say so plainly and help you find one.

What happens on the free 15-minute call?

You tell me where you walked and what you came home with. I tell you honestly whether I think six weeks with me would help. If the answer is no, I will say that too.
